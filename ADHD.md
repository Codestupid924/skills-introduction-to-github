# Understanding ADHD

## What is ADHD?

**ADHD (Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder)** is a neurodevelopmental condition that affects both children and adults. It is characterized by persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and/or impulsivity that interfere with daily functioning and development.

## Types of ADHD

There are three main presentations of ADHD:

1. **Predominantly Inattentive Presentation** – difficulty paying attention, staying organized, and following through on tasks.
2. **Predominantly Hyperactive-Impulsive Presentation** – excessive movement, talking, and difficulty waiting or sitting still.
3. **Combined Presentation** – a combination of both inattentive and hyperactive-impulsive symptoms.

## Common Symptoms

### Inattention
- Difficulty sustaining focus on tasks or activities
- Easily distracted by unrelated thoughts or stimuli
- Forgetting daily tasks and losing items frequently
- Trouble following through on instructions
- Avoiding tasks that require prolonged mental effort

### Hyperactivity & Impulsivity
- Fidgeting, tapping, or squirming
- Difficulty remaining seated in appropriate situations
- Talking excessively
- Interrupting others or blurting out answers
- Difficulty waiting for a turn

## Causes and Risk Factors

ADHD is believed to result from a combination of:
- **Genetics** – ADHD often runs in families
- **Brain structure and function** – differences in areas responsible for attention and impulse control
- **Environmental factors** – prenatal exposure to tobacco, alcohol, or certain toxins

## Diagnosis

ADHD is diagnosed through a comprehensive evaluation that may include:
- Clinical interviews and behavioral rating scales
- Medical history review
- Input from parents, teachers, or caregivers
- Ruling out other conditions

## Treatment Options

ADHD is highly treatable. Common approaches include:

- **Medication** – Stimulants (e.g., methylphenidate, amphetamines) and non-stimulants are often effective.
- **Behavioral therapy** – Helps develop coping strategies and organizational skills.
- **Parent training and education** – Supports families in managing ADHD-related challenges.
- **School accommodations** – Extended test time, preferential seating, and structured environments.
- **Lifestyle strategies** – Regular exercise, consistent routines, adequate sleep, and a healthy diet.

## Living with ADHD

Many people with ADHD lead successful, fulfilling lives. Strengths often associated with ADHD include:
- High creativity and out-of-the-box thinking
- Hyperfocus on areas of deep interest
- Energetic and enthusiastic approach to life
- Resilience and adaptability
